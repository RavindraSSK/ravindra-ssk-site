01 / Method

The OPT model, phase by phase.

NASM builds capacity in order — stability earns strength, strength earns power. Select a phase to read its prescription.

Earn control before load

Movement quality, joint stability, and endurance first. Higher reps under slow tempo — the base every later phase stands on.

Intensity
LOW · 12–20 reps
Tempo
SLOW · controlled

Hold control under load

Stable and heavy movements paired in supersets, so newly built strength keeps the control that phase one taught it.

Intensity
MODERATE · 8–12 reps
Structure
SUPERSET PAIRS

Build what strength will use

Hypertrophy focus. Accumulate volume to build the tissue that maximal strength and power will later express.

Intensity
MODERATE–HIGH · 6–12
Volume
HIGH · short rest

Raise the ceiling

Heavy load, low reps, full recovery. Trains the nervous system to recruit force — the ceiling that power sits on top of.

Intensity
HIGH · 1–5 reps
Rest
LONG · full recovery

Make it fast

Move well-owned patterns quickly. Rate of force development — strength made explosive, and the phase that sits closest to sport.

03 / Inputs

Recovery is programmed, not left over.

Sleep

7H MINIMUM

The single input with the widest effect. A short night lowers the next day's load — that's a rule, not a preference.

Fuel

REGULAR MEALS

Consistent meals on long research days. I don't run experiments hungry, and I don't lift hungry either.

Reset

1 LIGHT DAY / WEEK

Walks, stretching, and one deliberately light day before heavy weeks. It keeps the whole thing repeatable.

04 / Origin

It started on a handball court.

13

Sprint, jump, throw, reset. Repeat for sixty minutes.

Handball is where the habit came from. It's a game of short hard efforts stitched together by recovery — which is, more or less, the argument this whole page is making.

It also taught the part that has nothing to do with fitness: individual capacity only counts if it holds up inside a system other people depend on.
